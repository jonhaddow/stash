# Decisions

- **Replaces Readkit, Instapaper and Inoreader** (October 2026): one app, its own D1 database (not much data), for one person.
- **Two tabs, Later and Feeds** (October 2026): in Readkit, moving between the Inoreader and Instapaper sides took 4 or 5 taps. Here it's one.
- **Swipes, not buttons** (October 2026, replacing per-row buttons): in Feeds, swipe right marks an entry read and swipe left saves it to Later; either way it leaves the list, with Undo (see the inbox entry below). With read ones shown, swipe right toggles read in place and swipe left on a saved one ("In Later") takes it out. In Later, swipe right archives, with Undo. Opening a link never archives it. What a swipe will do shows under the row as it slides, turning amber past the point where letting go does it. First tried buttons, since Readkit's swipes were hard to remember, but the buttons took room and swipes are quicker.
- **Links open the original page; no article extraction** (October 2026): Jon reads on the site (Hacker News threads in the Hack app, below). Also keeps the Worker well inside the free plan's CPU.
- **Thumbnails beside titles** (October 2026), in Feeds, Later and the archive: from the feed for entries, from the page's `og:image` for saved links (one saved from Feeds keeps its entry's). Only the URL is stored (https only) and the image loads from its own site, with no proxy. YouTube videos without one use the video's own thumbnail; rows without one, or whose image fails to load, show just the title.
- **YouTube videos play in the app** (October 2026): tapping one opens YouTube's embedded player (youtube-nocookie.com) over the list, so Jon stays in Stash; closing it stops the video, and a button opens it on YouTube instead. Other video sites still open their page.
- **Articles and videos are filtered apart**, on both tabs: different reading sessions. Decided by the link's site (`kind.ts`: YouTube, Vimeo and so on).
- **Feeds have read/unread, but nothing to tick off** (October 2026): opening an entry marks it read; unread ones are bold with a dot. The list shows unread entries by default (Jon's call): turning off the Unread chip shows read ones too, and with none left it says "All caught up." rather than falling back to everything. One opened stays in place until the list reloads. Entries are never removed. "Mark all read" is there if wanted, and stops at when the page loaded. A feed added in the app starts with entries older than a week read, so adding one doesn't flood the list.
- **Feeds are an inbox** (October 2026, replacing "a separate list, not a river to triage"): every entry gets dealt with, read (swipe right) or saved to Later (swipe left, which marks it read too), and leaves the unread list either way, with Undo (which also takes it back out of Later). No new column: an entry's `readAt` is "dealt with", and whether it's in Later is a join on its URL.
- **Folders one level deep** (YouTube channels, blogs, Reddit), made while adding or editing a feed. They organise feeds in Settings only: the Feeds tab is one list, with no folder filters (Jon didn't use them; October 2026).
- **Archiving goes to a hidden archive** (Settings → Archive), not deleted; it'll also hold the Instapaper history.
- **Share sheet through an iOS Shortcut** (October 2026): iOS doesn't let home-screen web apps receive shares (no Web Share Target). The Shortcut posts the URL to `/api/save` with a token made in Settings (only its hash is stored; making a new one replaces it). JSON only, since SvelteKit's CSRF check refuses outside form posts.
- **Polling on the free plan** (October 2026): a cron every 5 minutes fetches the 5 feeds fetched longest ago, if not fetched in the last 30 minutes. Requests are conditional (ETag/Last-Modified), so unchanged feeds cost almost nothing. Waiting on the network doesn't count towards the ~10 ms CPU limit; parsing does, so feeds are parsed with regexes rather than an XML library. With more than about 25 feeds, each one is fetched less often than every 30 minutes; Workers Paid ($5/month) is the fix if that ever matters.
- **Sync log per feed**: every fetch (status, HTTP code, entries added, error, time), kept 30 days.
- **Reddit through its signed-in API** (October 2026): Reddit's public `.rss` is rate-limited per address, and Cloudflare's addresses are shared with every other Worker, so about 2 in 3 requests got 429s (one feed per run and retrying on the next run helped, but only by luck). Jon's existing Reddit app (app-only sign-in, no Reddit login) gets its own quota of 1,000 requests per 10 minutes. Feeds are still stored as their `.rss` URLs (subreddit links go straight to `/r/<name>/.rss`); `reddit.ts` sends those requests to `oauth.reddit.com` and hands the listing back as Atom with the same ids and links as Reddit's own feed, so nothing else changed and nothing was seen twice. Without the secrets (local development), `.rss` as before. The one-Reddit-feed-per-run and retry-a-429 rules stay, as a backstop. Turned down: fetching Reddit every 3 hours (fewer tries, not better ones), fetching it from the laptop (only when it's on).
- **Its own look**: a newspaper. Serif headlines and titles (the device's Iowan Old Style or Palatino), Manrope for the rest, newsprint and ink, deep amber for actions and unread. Hairline rules instead of cards, square corners, filters as underlined words, a tab bar docked along the bottom (a masthead along the top on a laptop). Follows the device's light or dark setting.
- **No push notifications** for new entries: push only carries the icon badge's count (below).
- **Unread count on the app icon** (October 2026): the Badging API, set from the layout's unread count whenever the app is open or comes back to the front. iOS shows it only once notifications are allowed (a button in Settings). Kept current while the app is closed by Web Push from the cron (October 2026): after each sync, a changed unread count goes to each subscribed device, whose service worker sets the badge. iOS allows no silent pushes, so each one also shows a notification (one tag, so they replace each other); Jon turns off its banners and lock screen in iOS. A count of 0 isn't pushed; opening the app clears the badge. Push is done with WebCrypto (`push.ts`, checked against RFC 8291's example) rather than a library.
- **Imports are one-off scripts**, not a Settings page (October 2026): `scripts/import-opml.ts` writes SQL for `wrangler d1 execute`. Imported feeds start with everything read (Jon's call): their first fetch marks all existing entries read, so only new ones show as unread.
- **"[video]" in a title makes it a video** (October 2026): Hacker News marks talks and other videos that way, so they go with YouTube links under Videos (`kindOf` takes the title too). They open the link as before; only YouTube plays in the app. A migration fixed the rows already there.
- **Newest or oldest first, per list** (October 2026): a dropdown on Later and on Feeds, remembered in a cookie each (`order-later`, `order-feeds`) so the server sorts. Feeds pages on with `?from=`, older or newer by the order.
- **The Instapaper history came in as a one-off** (October 2026): Buttondown tracking links were unwrapped to the Hacker News threads, and archived links were given their saved date as `archivedAt` (Instapaper's export doesn't have one).
- **Other looks as experiments** (October 2026): Settings → Experimental: look offers mock-ups to try for real on the phone and the laptop: Desk (edge-to-edge rows and a docked tab bar; a sidebar on a laptop), Magazine (picture cards; a grid on a laptop) and Paper (serif and rules; tabs along the top on a laptop). Each is CSS only (`routes/looks.css`, keyed on `data-look`), per device (a cookie). Paper was picked and folded into `app.css` (with square corners everywhere); the others were dropped.
- **Hacker News threads open in Hack on iOS** (October 2026): a `news.ycombinator.com/item?id=` link opens `hack://item?id=` on an iPhone or iPad (decided on tap, by the user agent), and the page as before on the laptop. Only links to the thread itself: an HN feed entry linking to the article still opens the article.
